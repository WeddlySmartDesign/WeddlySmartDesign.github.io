# GUEST — Competitive direction checkpoint — 2026-10-02

Status: SEALED for market direction and pilot gate.

## Scope boundary
- Work only on GUEST isolated from ONE, ONE Partner and STUDIO.
- ONE is already commercial and is not part of the current GUEST design work.
- GUEST engine/app is functionally ready enough for now and remains in standby.
- Current priority is the invitation itself: visual impact, art direction, motion, music, personalization and premium perception.

## Project operating rule
1. Research the market deeply.
2. Extract the best decisions from each relevant competitor and identify gaps/frictions.
3. Build GUEST from the best available ideas plus a clear improvement competitors do not cover.
4. If GUEST cannot meet or beat the market standard as a whole, stop the project.
5. Do not reduce the quality gate because of fatigue, speed or convenience.
6. The owner should not have to design; her role is mobile review and light directional feedback only.
7. Do not buy new paid design tools before the pilot proves a concrete need.

## Microblock 01 — MARKET MAP — SEALED
Reference groups:
- Premium semi-personalized / service-led: La Qualité, The Digital Yes, The Digital Invite, The Sealed Invite.
- Premium self-service / cinematic: Join Wedding, InviteVault, Zinggly, Invitation RSVP.
- Large-catalog / design + function: Fixdate, Joy, Paperless Post, Bliss & Bone.
- Low-cost functional: Fiestly, Enlázate and similar products.

La Qualité is a useful benchmark, not the sole benchmark.

## Microblock 02 — BEST-OF + GAP — SEALED
The strongest recurring market ideas are:
- a memorable opening/reveal;
- motion and music that make the invitation feel like an experience rather than a webpage;
- a reusable art-directed template system that looks highly personalized after inserting couple-specific content;
- built-in RSVP and guest management;
- mobile-first experience;
- guided setup instead of requiring design skills from the couple.

The main gap GUEST should target:
- premium visual quality maintained from first frame to final frame;
- opening that is not forced to use the now-common animated envelope;
- restrained editorial interior instead of overloaded decoration;
- photos integrated into the art direction rather than pasted into decorative shapes;
- RSVP visually integrated into the chosen design, not a generic form dropped into the page;
- a designed closing scene / final animation as part of the standard experience;
- deep apparent personalization on top of reusable systems;
- done-for-you personalization rather than a self-service design editor;
- the GUEST management engine stays behind the purchase decision and becomes a post-purchase value discovery.

Core test:
> The invitation must clearly feel like something a couple could not simply reproduce by themselves in Canva.
