# GUEST — END-TO-END CUSTOMER EXPERIENCE STANDARD

Date: 2026-10-07
Status: SEALED PRINCIPLE / IMPLEMENTATION GATE

## Core principle

A couple may choose GUEST because they like the invitation design and the price. They must not be expected to know the WeddlySmartDesign brand, the internal GUEST architecture, or what happens after purchase.

The experience must explain itself.

The product is judged as one continuous journey:
1. discover designs;
2. understand what is included;
3. choose and buy;
4. provide content;
5. review and request changes;
6. receive and share the final invitation;
7. discover and use the included guest-management tools;
8. manage replies, seating, logistics and lists.

A failure at any step can damage the overall product experience even if the invitation design itself is excellent.

## Mandatory UX rules

- Never assume prior knowledge of GUEST or WeddlySmartDesign.
- Never use internal/process language such as “this is not a template”, “send to WeddlySmartDesign”, environment/test terminology, or implementation details in customer-facing copy.
- Every transition must answer visually and plainly:
  - what just happened;
  - what the couple should do now;
  - what happens next.
- Guidance must be visual wherever an action or flow could be misunderstood.
- Important features must not remain hidden merely because they are technically available.
- The invitation is the acquisition product; management capability is discovered progressively after purchase/delivery.
- Premium means both visual quality and frictionless, reassuring service.

## Journey gates before public launch

### 1. Browse / choose
Couple must understand:
- what each design looks like;
- what can be personalised;
- what is included in the purchase;
- what happens after purchase.

### 2. Purchase
Couple must know:
- what they are buying;
- what they will receive next;
- when action is required from them.

### 3. Questionnaire
- visual reference for every relevant invitation section;
- plain-language fields;
- no internal terminology;
- no ambiguity about optional vs required information.

### 4. Order received
Confirmation email must show the process visually:
data received -> preparation -> review -> final delivery.

### 5. Review
Review email/screen must show:
- what to check;
- how to approve;
- how to request changes;
- no need to infer where changes should be sent.

### 6. Final delivery / sharing
Delivery must explicitly teach:
- how to open;
- how to share the invitation link;
- not to forward the delivery email instead of the invitation link.

### 7. Management discovery
The couple must be told clearly that their purchase also includes guest management and what it can do:
- responses;
- guest data;
- +1 / children / menu and other collected information where enabled;
- transport / accommodation information;
- table plan and seating;
- moving/seating multiple guests where supported;
- lists/export/operational views.

This should use visual onboarding, not a dense feature list.

### 8. In-product onboarding
First open of management should not be an empty dashboard. It should guide the couple through the next useful actions and surface features progressively.

## Email copy direction

Subjects should describe the customer event, not internal product branding:
- Hemos recibido vuestros datos
- Vuestra invitación está lista para revisar
- Vuestra invitación está lista para compartir

Customer-facing branding may still appear visually inside the email, but “GUEST” is not required in the subject.
